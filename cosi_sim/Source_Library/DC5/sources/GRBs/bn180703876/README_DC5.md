## How the DC5 values were chosen

- **Time:** all DC5 GRBs fall in days 4-7 of the observation (3-7 days after the start of the DC4 orientation file), so they overlap the week of background simulations with ACS data and come after the activation build-up. The whole burst plus a 5 min margin ends before 1836092100.0 (7 days after the start of the orientation file). The time is random within the window, with >= 1 h separation from any other DC5 GRB.
- **Position:** placed at a target off-axis angle (angle between the COSI z-axis and the source) of 23.2 deg, at a random azimuth around the z-axis at the trigger time. The targets are the DC4 GRB off-axis values jittered by up to +-3 deg and randomly reassigned, so the DC5 set reproduces the DC4 distribution. Required: off-axis angle <= 60 deg during the whole burst, zenith angle <= 100 deg (well above the ~113 deg Earth-occultation limit), livetime > 0 (no SAA) from 5 min before to 5 min after the burst, and an off-axis change < 3 deg during the burst (no slew).
- **Flux:** DC4 photon flux (100 keV-10 MeV, time-averaged over the light curve) times a log-uniform factor in [0.5, 2]. For the two DC4 bursts above 100 ph/cm2/s, which are already artificially bright, the factor is in [0.5, 1].
- **Spectrum:** same spectral model as DC4; parameters drawn from the Fermi GBM 10-year catalog fluence-spectrum distributions (long GRBs): alpha and log10(Epeak) Gaussian around the catalog medians (long: alpha = -0.84, Epeak = 144 keV; short: alpha = -0.46, Epeak = 413 keV), beta Gaussian around -2.3 (sigma 0.3), with cuts to keep alpha > beta and Epeak in a range COSI can constrain. For power-law bursts the index follows the high-energy (beta) distribution, since COSI only sees the spectrum above 100 keV.
- **Polarization:** the DC4 GRB polarization fractions are spread uniformly between 0 and 0.9; DC5 fractions are drawn from the same distribution (stratified uniform on [0, 0.9]). The angle is uniform in [0, 180) deg.



## MEGAlib simulation inputs (times relative to t_ref)

MEGAlib has trouble simulating short bursts at large absolute unix times, so this burst is simulated in times relative to **t_ref = 1835487300.0** (the first time of the DC4/DC5 orientation file):

- `bn180703876_lightcurve_shifted.dat`: the light curve with time − t_ref; the source file points to it. The original light curve with absolute times, `bn180703876_lightcurve.dat`, is kept for reference.
- `DC3_final_530km_3_month_with_slew_1sbins_GalacticEarth_cut_normed.ori`: the 22 orientation rows covering the burst, time − t_ref (412632–412653 s). This is the orientation file for the Cosima run. `DC3_final_530km_3_month_with_slew_1sbins_GalacticEarth_cut.ori` holds the same rows with absolute times.
- `sim_time_reference.txt`: t_ref and the burst window, absolute (1835899932.874–1835899952.326) and shifted (412632.874–412652.326).

After the Cosima run, add t_ref to the simulated event times to return to the absolute times of the orientation file. Generated by `scripts/make_sim_time_inputs.py`.
